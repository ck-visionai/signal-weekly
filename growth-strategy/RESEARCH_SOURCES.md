# Subscriber Growth Research Notes

Research gathered on 24 August 2026 for the Signal Weekly subscriber-acquisition strategy.

| Source | Relevant finding for Signal Weekly |
|---|---|
| [Compounding Marketing — Newsletter Growth](https://cmo-skills.com/skills/newsletter-growth) | Recommends a clear value proposition, minimal email-only form, contextual content upgrades, partnership distribution and measurement of subscriber quality—not just list size. Treats checklist, template and guide formats as low-to-medium effort lead magnets suited to quick, practical wins. |
| [Inbox Collective — lead-magnet case study](https://inboxcollective.com/how-i-used-a-lead-magnet-to-grow-my-email-list-by-22-percent/) | A single case study, not a universal benchmark. It demonstrates the importance of a narrowly relevant original resource, a pre-launch waitlist, multi-channel launch distribution, creator collaborations and repeated promotion. It also reports that an expensive LinkedIn-ad experiment produced few sign-ups, so paid acquisition should not be the first priority for Signal Weekly. |
| [Typeform — lead magnet guidance](https://www.typeform.com/blog/want-more-email-signups-try-lead-magnets) | Describes lead magnets as free value exchanged for contact details. It emphasizes relevance, credibility, cross-channel sharing and a clear welcome sequence. It cites third-party conversion and ROI figures that should not be treated as a forecast for Signal Weekly. |
| [MailerLite — lead magnet guide](https://www.mailerlite.com/blog/lead-magnet-examples-and-ideas) | Supports specific, actionable, credible lead magnets; clear landing-page calls to action; delivery after confirmation; ongoing testing; segmentation only where it improves the reader experience; and regular review of conversion and engagement. |

## Practical implications

1. Begin with a specific, immediately useful career-decision tool—not a broad “newsletter updates” promise.
2. Make the resource legible before the form: show a preview, state who it helps and name the practical outcome.
3. Use LinkedIn thought leadership, targeted partner outreach and contextual links from resource pages before considering paid acquisition.
4. Do not invent social proof, subscriber counts, testimonials or performance claims. Use real evidence as it becomes available.
5. Measure signups by source, confirmation rate, welcome-email delivery, engagement and unsubscribes before increasing distribution spend.
