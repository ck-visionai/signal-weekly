# Signal Weekly — Landing Page Design Direction

## Three Possible Directions

### 1. Executive Signal Desk
**Very Brief Intro:** A refined editorial desk inspired by high-end financial and technology publications. It turns job-search guidance into intelligent, composed career intelligence for leaders.

**Probability:** 0.06

### 2. Quiet Momentum
**Very Brief Intro:** A soft, humanist product aesthetic using warm paper whites and velvety gradients. It frames career change as a measured next chapter rather than an urgent scramble.

**Probability:** 0.03

### 3. Applied Advantage
**Very Brief Intro:** A crisp, analytical system of index cards, fine rules, and measured typography. It makes the newsletter feel like a private briefing built for a considered search.

**Probability:** 0.08

## Chosen Direction: Executive Signal Desk

### Design Movement
**Contemporary editorial minimalism** informed by the composure of premium business publishing, Apple’s clean material restraint, and Stripe’s confident color logic. It should feel like an invitation to a weekly private briefing, not a generic email-capture page.

### Core Principles
1. **Calm authority:** Generous whitespace and precise hierarchy let high-value career advice feel credible and unhurried.
2. **Tactile clarity:** Fine graphite rules, elevated paper cards, and soft light convey a considered editorial object.
3. **Purposeful asymmetry:** The hero pairs a left-aligned proposition with a slightly off-axis newsletter artifact rather than a centered conversion block.
4. **Signal over noise:** One deep blue brand accent and restrained micro-interactions direct attention to the subscription decision.

### Color Philosophy
Warm ivory creates the calm, high-touch foundation associated with quality paper and focused thought. Graphite gives text the decisiveness expected by senior professionals. A deep cobalt-ink accent communicates trust, momentum, and practical intelligence, while pale ice-blue and apricot glows add the softly dimensional polish requested without turning the page into a saturated gradient treatment.

### Layout Paradigm
The page is composed as a **sequence of editorial spreads**. A generous hero occupies a broad left column while the newsletter artifact floats into the right edge. The value section uses overlapping “briefing cards,” sample issues cascade like a document tray, and the closing subscription panel returns to the calm of a personal desk. The layout avoids a single centered column and keeps visual weight moving down the page.

### Signature Elements
- A **cobalt signal line** that marks labels, dividers, and active controls.
- Softly lit **orbital gradient fields** behind high-value moments, used sparingly.
- A **document artifact motif** featuring paper-like brief cards with coded issue markers and precise metadata.

### Interaction Philosophy
Interactions are discreet and assured. Buttons compress slightly on press, cards rise just enough on hover to suggest a stacked paper object, and carousel navigation changes with a short, crisp slide. The signup state should answer immediately with an in-page acknowledgement instead of a disruptive modal.

### Animation
At initial load, the hero copy and newsletter artifact enter in an 80ms stagger using opacity and a small vertical translation. Cards use a 180ms cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1) transition for hover. Ambient gradient fields drift almost imperceptibly only where motion preferences allow. All decorative motion is removed for `prefers-reduced-motion`.

### Typography System
**DM Serif Display** carries key editorial moments, with a restrained, intelligent contrast for the hero and section titles. **Manrope** supports navigation, interface elements, and body copy with highly legible, modern geometry. Headlines use a compact 0.98 letter-spacing relationship at large sizes; labels are uppercase Manrope with expanded tracking; body copy uses generous line height and never drops below 15px.

### Brand Essence
**Signal Weekly is the weekly career intelligence briefing for experienced professionals positioning themselves for their next consequential role.**

Personality: **clear-headed, discreet, exacting**.

### Brand Voice
Headlines are direct and composed; CTAs tell the reader precisely what they receive; microcopy favors evidence and cadence over hype.

Example headline: “A sharper search starts with a better signal.”

Example CTA: “Send me Friday’s briefing.”

### Wordmark & Logo
The mark is a compact **three-bar signal glyph**: three offset cobalt vertical marks whose ascending heights suggest increasing clarity and upward professional trajectory. The wordmark is set in a custom-feeling high-weight Manrope construction beside it, with a small cobalt point after “Weekly.” The glyph alone is used as the favicon.

### Signature Brand Color
**Signal Cobalt — #1646D8**

## Content Decisions

The newsletter is positioned as a weekly, practical briefing for mid-career and executive job seekers. Its recurring pillars are **ATS-forward resume strategy**, **executive-level interview preparation**, and **compensation negotiation**. The sample content is intentionally framed as editorial previews. Because verified subscriber testimonials have not been supplied, the requested testimonial carousel will instead become a **“The Briefing, In Practice” topic carousel** that previews three reader-relevant situations without presenting fabricated subscriber claims.

## Style Decisions

- The final subscription moment uses an asymmetric navy briefing artifact on a warm paper field rather than a centered cobalt-gradient banner.
- Signal Cobalt is reserved for primary calls to action, issue markers, fine signal rules, and selected editorial emphasis.
- Secondary value-panel visuals favor paper, metadata, and document artifacts over generic symbolic business photography.
- The wordmark uses high-weight Manrope with a cobalt terminal point; the three-bar glyph remains its distinctive companion mark.
- Cobalt fine rules act as a repeating editorial index across labels, card markers, dividers, and primary actions.
- Each major spread includes a briefing-object cue such as coded metadata, an issue index, document layering, or a signal rule.
