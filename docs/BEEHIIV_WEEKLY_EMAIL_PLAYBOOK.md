# Career Signal: Simple Beehiiv Weekly Email Playbook

**Purpose:** This is the practical owner guide for preparing, scheduling, checking, and improving Career Signal’s weekly subscriber email. It is written for a non-technical editor.

> **Important:** Scheduling an email does not write the weekly content for you. It only tells Beehiiv *when* to send a completed email. Keep ownership of every edition: draft it, preview it, test it, then schedule it.

This guide does **not** publish the existing Beehiiv form, Welcome Email, or any newsletter. Those remain owner-controlled drafts until you explicitly decide to publish them.

---

## 1. The simple approach to use now

For Career Signal, use **one normal scheduled newsletter post each Friday**. This is the safest and most useful starting method because every weekly issue needs original, reviewed career guidance and one carefully selected resource.

| Choice | What happens | Best for Career Signal now? | Why |
|---|---|---:|---|
| **Scheduled weekly post** | You prepare one newsletter, choose the Friday time, and Beehiiv sends it automatically at that time. | **Yes — use this now.** | You keep editorial control of every issue while removing the risk of forgetting to send it. |
| **Single Welcome Email** | Beehiiv sends one message automatically to a newly confirmed subscriber. | **Yes, once you approve the existing draft.** | It sets expectations and points new readers to the free working page. |
| **Multi-email welcome journey** | Beehiiv sends several emails over days or weeks after a subscriber joins. | **Later, if needed.** | Useful when you have several evergreen resources and want a structured introduction. |
| **Fully recurring RSS email series** | Beehiiv reuses a recurring format and refreshes an RSS feed before every send. | **Not recommended now.** | Your Career Signal reader editions need human review; recurring series are mainly for feed-driven content and are an Enterprise feature. |

> **Recommended rhythm:** Draft on Monday or Tuesday, review on Wednesday, schedule by Thursday, and let Beehiiv send on Friday at your chosen time in the correct time zone.

---

## 2. Your weekly routine at a glance

| When | Your action | Time needed |
|---|---|---:|
| **Monday** | Pick the week’s career decision and working resource. | 15–30 minutes |
| **Tuesday** | Write the email and add the resource link. | 30–60 minutes |
| **Wednesday** | Preview it on desktop and mobile; send yourself a test email. | 10–15 minutes |
| **Thursday** | Confirm the subscriber audience, subject line, and Friday schedule. | 5–10 minutes |
| **Friday** | Check the sent email and its web version after the scheduled time. | 5 minutes |
| **Following week** | Review the dashboard results and write down one improvement. | 10 minutes |

Choose **one stable Friday time** and keep it consistent. Since Career Signal is operated from Singapore, a sensible default is **Friday, 9:00 AM, Asia/Singapore (GMT+8)**. You may change this later after you have enough results to compare times.

## 3. How to schedule one weekly email

Use this exact sequence every week. The names may appear slightly differently if Beehiiv updates its interface, but the order remains the same: **Compose → Audience → Email → Web → Review**.[1]

### Step 1 — Start a fresh draft

1. Log in to Beehiiv and make sure you are in the **Career Signal** publication.
2. In the left menu, select **Posts**.
3. Click **New**, then choose **Blank draft post**. Once your layout is settled, you can instead choose **Template post** to reuse the same Career Signal structure.[2]
4. Give the internal draft a clear working title, such as `Career Signal — 12 September — ATS proof points`.

### Step 2 — Write the edition in Compose

1. Add a clear reader-facing title.
2. Start with one short paragraph explaining the career problem.
3. Give one practical framework, example, checklist, or exercise.
4. Add **one primary call to action**. For example: `Open this week’s working page →`.
5. Add the correct Career Signal resource link. Use the one complete free working page or the approved scheduled reader edition; do not promise a full locked edition unless it is actually included.
6. Save the draft. Do not click any send or publish button yet.

### Step 3 — Choose the right audience

1. Click **Next**, or open the **Audience** tab.
2. Under **Email audience**, choose your active free subscribers. Do not include test, imported, paid, or special segments unless that is your deliberate plan.
3. Under **Web audience**, choose whether you also want a web version. For a normal weekly edition, leaving both email and web selected is usually the clearest approach.
4. If you are unsure, stop here and review. A wrong audience is more serious than a late send.

### Step 4 — Set the subject line and preview text

1. Open the **Email** tab.
2. Write a direct subject line that describes the reader benefit. Example: `The proof behind a stronger résumé claim`.
3. Add preview text that completes the promise. Example: `A five-minute method for turning scope into evidence.`
4. Leave A/B testing off until you have a stable process and enough subscribers to make the comparison useful.
5. Keep the normal **Read Online** link unless you have a deliberate reason to send readers elsewhere.[3]

### Step 5 — Check the web version

1. Open the **Web** tab.
2. Check the title, URL, and featured image.
3. Confirm that any resource links open the correct Career Signal page or approved PDF.
4. Keep the post readable even if someone views it in a browser rather than their inbox.

### Step 6 — Preview and send yourself a test

1. Open the **Review** tab.
2. Confirm the audience summary, title, subject line, preview text, and web settings.
3. Click **Preview** to check both the email and web presentation.
4. Use the arrow beside **Preview** to send a test email to yourself.
5. Open the test on your phone as well as your computer. Check every link, the resource download, spelling, spacing, and the unsubscribe/footer area.

> Do not use a live subscriber’s address for testing unless it is your own address. A test message should not become an accidental subscriber send.

### Step 7 — Schedule Friday’s send

1. Still on **Review**, click **Schedule** at the bottom.
2. Select **Schedule for later**.
3. Choose the upcoming **Friday**.
4. Enter your preferred send time — start with **9:00 AM**.
5. Confirm the time zone says **Asia/Singapore** if that is your operating time zone.
6. Re-read the audience and subject line one last time.
7. Click the final schedule button. The post should now show as scheduled, not as sent.

Beehiiv’s Review page is the final check before a post is scheduled or sent. It also lets you edit audience choices, post details, and subject details before you commit.[1]

## 4. The five-point safety check before every send

Before scheduling, answer **yes** to all five questions.

| Check | Yes / No |
|---|---|
| Is the email going only to the intended subscriber audience? | ☐ |
| Does the subject line accurately describe the email? | ☐ |
| Does every link work and go to the correct resource? | ☐ |
| Did I receive and read a test email on both computer and phone? | ☐ |
| Is the Friday date, time, and time zone correct? | ☐ |

If any answer is **no**, do not schedule. Return to the relevant tab, correct it, and repeat the check.

---

## 5. What to do after Friday’s email sends

1. Open Beehiiv shortly after the scheduled time.
2. Confirm the post shows **Sent** or **Published**, rather than Draft or Scheduled.
3. Open the web version and click the main resource link once.
4. Check that the email arrived in your own inbox.
5. Do not judge results immediately. Give readers at least 48–72 hours before interpreting opens or clicks.
6. On the next Wednesday, write down only three figures: sends/deliveries, unique opens, and unique clicks. Then note one lesson for the next issue.

> A lower open rate does not automatically mean the content was poor. First check whether the subject line was clear, the send time was consistent, and the main link was easy to find.

## 6. The automation to use now: one Welcome Email

Your simple signup automation is a **single Welcome Email**. It is separate from the weekly Friday newsletter.

When enabled, Beehiiv sends it automatically to every new subscriber, or after confirmation if double opt-in is turned on. A single Welcome Email is available on all Beehiiv plans.[4]

### Recommended Welcome Email job

1. Welcome the reader to Career Signal.
2. Explain that they will receive one practical Friday brief.
3. Link to the complete free `Evidence Before Adjectives` working page or the Sample Library destination.
4. Ask the reader to add your sender email to their contacts.

### Important: avoid duplicate welcomes

If you later create a multi-email **Welcome Automation**, first switch off the separate Welcome Email. Otherwise, new subscribers may receive two welcome messages.[4]

## 7. Optional later step: a three-email welcome journey

Use this only after the simple Welcome Email is working well and you have at least three helpful evergreen messages ready. Beehiiv calls this an **Automation** or automated journey. It uses a subscriber event, such as `Signed Up`, followed by email steps and time delays.[5]

| Timing after signup | Suggested message | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Immediately** | Welcome + free working page | Deliver the promised first value. |
| **3 days later** | How to use the working page | Help the reader apply it rather than merely download it. |
| **7 days later** | Best next Career Signal resource | Encourage the reader to look forward to Friday’s regular briefing. |

### How to create it later

1. In Beehiiv, open **Automations**.
2. Select **Create automation**, then choose a Quick Start template or **New automation**.[5]
3. Choose the **Signed Up** trigger for brand-new subscribers.
4. Add a **Send email** action.
5. Add a **Time delay**, then a second Send email action.
6. Name the workflow clearly, for example `Career Signal — New Subscriber Welcome`.
7. Keep re-entry set to **No re-entry** at the start, so the same person is not sent the sequence repeatedly.
8. Read every email and check every link before clicking **Publish**.

Automations are a paid Beehiiv feature. Check the plan label inside your account before spending time building one.[4] [5]

---

## 8. Do not use fully recurring email series yet

Beehiiv offers a recurring email series that can send on a daily, weekly, or monthly cadence, but it is an **Enterprise** feature and is designed to refresh RSS-feed content automatically at send time.[6]

Do **not** use it for Career Signal at this stage. It would be too easy to send an edition before its wording, links, resource permissions, and reader value have been reviewed. Your scheduled weekly-post process gives you the same reliable Friday rhythm while preserving editorial control.

## 9. Simple naming system to stay organised

Use these names inside Beehiiv so you can find things later.

| Item | Naming pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly draft | `Career Signal — YYYY-MM-DD — Topic` | `Career Signal — 2026-09-12 — Interview evidence` |
| Welcome Email | `Career Signal — Welcome Email` | `Career Signal — Welcome Email` |
| Future welcome journey | `Career Signal — New Subscriber Welcome` | `Career Signal — New Subscriber Welcome` |
| Resource file | `Sample Briefing 0X — Title` | `Sample Briefing 02 — The ATS Translation Layer` |

---

## 10. Your first practical action

For the next Friday send, do only this:

1. Create **one** new draft.
2. Write one useful brief and link one approved resource.
3. Send yourself a test.
4. Schedule it for Friday.
5. Check that it sent.

That is a complete and professional weekly-email system. Add further automation only once this routine feels easy and reliable.
